# NewBatteries Portable Battery Identification and Safe Disposal Pilot

**Pilot concept for discussion with the Battery Stewardship Council / B-cycle**

## The problem

Consumers, retailers and frontline staff are often required to make battery decisions using incomplete, inconsistent or easily confused information.

Batteries with similar dimensions or markings may differ in chemistry, voltage, rechargeability, application, safety requirements and appropriate recycling pathway. A visual resemblance or partial model number is not always enough to establish identity.

This can lead to:

* incorrect battery identification;
* unsafe substitution or handling;
* disposal through the wrong stewardship pathway;
* avoidable contamination and fire risk;
* unnecessary reliance on unsupported assumptions; and
* loss of potentially useful product and stewardship data.

Existing search tools commonly prioritise producing an answer. NewBatteries is being designed to determine whether the available evidence is sufficient to support one.

## The proposed pilot

NewBatteries proposes a small, evidence-governed pilot using approximately **20–30 deliberately varied portable battery records**.

The test dataset would include:

* common household batteries;
* coin and button cells;
* rechargeable formats;
* removable tool and device batteries;
* visually similar batteries with important technical differences;
* incomplete or partially legible examples;
* conflicting manufacturer or supplier information;
* batteries requiring safety warnings; and
* examples that fall inside and outside the B-cycle collection pathway.

The pilot would test whether a deterministic identification system can distinguish:

1. **confirmed identity**;
2. **probable identity requiring confirmation**;
3. **multiple plausible identities**;
4. **conflicting evidence**;
5. **insufficient evidence**;
6. **compatibility not established**; and
7. **a safety or disposal issue requiring escalation**.

## What the system would produce

For each battery, the system would generate an auditable decision record containing:

* the proposed battery identity or identity family;
* the evidence supporting that conclusion;
* information that remains missing or uncertain;
* conflicts between sources or markings;
* chemistry, voltage and rechargeability warnings where relevant;
* a clear separation between **identity** and **device compatibility**;
* safe-handling prompts;
* the applicable recycling or specialist disposal pathway; and
* an explanation of why the result was reached.

The system would not claim certainty where the available evidence does not justify it.

## Pilot success measures

The pilot would be considered successful if it demonstrates that the model can:

* avoid false certainty;
* accurately distinguish similar battery identities;
* identify missing and conflicting evidence;
* separate battery identification from compatibility approval;
* surface safety-critical unknowns;
* correctly distinguish B-cycle and non-B-cycle pathways;
* provide a traceable explanation for every result; and
* identify where authoritative technical or stewardship data is still required.

## Value to battery stewardship

This pilot would explore whether evidence-governed battery identification could strengthen the point between **a person possessing an unknown battery** and **that battery entering the correct recovery pathway**.

Potential future applications include consumer guidance, retailer support, collection-point triage, education, digital recycling tools and improved visibility of commonly misidentified battery types.

This is not proposed as a replacement for B-cycle guidance, accredited participants or professional battery assessment. It is proposed as a possible decision-support layer that directs users toward the correct information, safety action and stewardship pathway.

## Proposed first step

NewBatteries seeks an initial discussion with the Battery Stewardship Council to determine:

* whether battery misidentification is a recognised stewardship or collection issue;
* whether the proposed pilot aligns with B-cycle’s safety, consumer-trust and circularity objectives;
* what authoritative data or classification controls should govern the pilot;
* whether B-cycle would provide limited technical feedback on the test design; and
* whether there may be value in a small co-designed validation exercise.
